# chiggy.ai

## Design Context

### Users
Hiring managers, recruiters, and senior/staff engineers evaluating Chirag Chadha for
software roles, plus inbound opportunity sources and LLM sourcing tools. They skim fast,
on desktop and mobile, and decide in seconds whether this person is exceptional. The site
must also be its own proof of craft: it is a work sample, not just a description of one.

### Brand Personality
Confident, sharp, builder-grade. Three words: **cracked, intentional, fast.** It should
feel like the personal site of one of the most technically capable engineers you have met,
who also has taste. Not corporate, not cute, not loud. Quietly elite.

### Aesthetic Direction
Refined-minimal pushed to its high-craft limit. Light theme (warm off-white, near-black
ink) consistent with the established brand. Hanken Grotesk for text, SF Mono for
labels/meta/keys (accent only, never body). Monochrome with a single living accent (the
green availability signal). Editorial, asymmetric, generous use of the full canvas, no dead
side gutters. Reference feel: anuragd.me, rauno.me, paco.me (bold typographic identity,
command palette, footer clock, density without clutter). Anti-references: generic centered
narrow-column template, AI-slop gradients/glassmorphism/neon, timid evenly-spaced layouts.

### Design Principles
1. **Use the whole canvas.** An asymmetric two-zone layout (a persistent identity rail and
   a scrolling content column) instead of one narrow centered column. Kill the side voids.
2. **One confident voice.** Big typographic hierarchy, strong contrast between steps, lots
   of monochrome, the accent earns attention by being rare.
3. **Motion with intent.** One orchestrated load (staggered reveals), a scroll-spy rail,
   spring-free smooth easing. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. No bounce, no decoration.
4. **Every surface responsive and intentional.** Spring-light hover feedback, real focus
   states, mobile adapts (rail collapses to a header) rather than amputating.
5. **The site is the resume.** It must pass the test: "how was this made?", never "which AI
   made this?"
