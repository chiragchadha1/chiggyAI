## What I did

Generate is Northeastern's student-led product development studio. I held three roles across
my time there: Software Project Lead, Content Lead, and Engagement Director.

As **Software Project Lead** I led a 15-person cross-functional team of engineers, designers,
and product managers to build **Couplet**, a social app connecting people through shared
interests in local events, from concept to a 300+ person Showcase.

As **Content Lead** I ran a rebranding and digital/SEO strategy that drove 103K+ impressions.

## Engagement Director

As **Engagement Director** I managed three chiefs and their teams across content, events, and
experiences for the 160+ person org. This was a people management role. I ran weekly team
meetings, held 1:1s with every lead and chief, and was responsible for hiring, event
planning, Showcase logistics, and org culture.

In the fall I noticed too much overlap between the Events and Experiences teams, and
communication kept breaking down when cross-team initiatives required both. I restructured
mid-year, merging them into a single Community team so initiatives like the Buddies Program,
cross-branch mixers, and one-off events could run under one roof. Mid-semester survey
results showed that members felt more connected to their branches compared to the prior year.

I ran hiring end to end. I sourced candidates proactively before applications even opened,
ran interviews conversationally rather than as a question checklist, and managed double-hire
conflicts where applicants applied across multiple branches. I also planned and ran social
events across fall and spring, handling venue scouting, budgeting, and logistics.

For events, I built an ideation framework with the team, a grid ranking every idea by
value, cost, and target audience. We deliberately cut down to five high-quality events per
semester instead of flooding people with invitations nobody tracked. We paired that with a
weekly calendar that went out to the whole org and started posting physical flyers in the
Sherman Center.

I also co-led Showcase planning across layout, catering, merchandise, and branding. We
hired a dedicated Showcase brand designer for the semester, which gave each Showcase its
own visual identity for the first time.

When I handed off the role to my successor, I did a full knowledge transfer covering team
structure, hiring process, event logistics, budget management, and org culture. The handoff
doc ran over 40 pages.

## Why it mattered

It is where I learned to lead, not just build. Scoping work for people with different
skills, shipping on a deadline, and mentoring a freshman who went on to run eight project
teams as Software Director. It turned a room full of students into a product org that
actually ships.
