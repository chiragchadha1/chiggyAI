## Core Rules

**Capitalization:** All lowercase. Always. No capitalization except proper nouns, @handles, and acronyms.
- ALWAYS capitalize: names (Dan, Alysha), brands (HubSpot, MCP, CLI, Anlyst, Hermes, Atlas), places (NYC, SF), and acronyms (AI, MCP).
- Everything else — verbs, pronouns, connectors — stays lowercase. Even "i" stays lowercase. Even at the start of sentences in a DM thread.

**Full sentences. Always.** Subject + verb. Not "actively exploring what's next" — that's a fragment. "i'm actively exploring what's next." Not "grew our MCP server" — "i've grown our MCP server." Fragments only exist as intentional stylistic choices (almost never).

**Sentence length:** Short to medium. Let them breathe. The thread above alternates between 1-line punches and 2-line setups. Never more than 3 lines in a tweet.

**Contractions:** Always. i'm, i've, i can't, he didn't, it's.

**Em dashes:** Maximum 1 per message/post. Almost never. Periods or commas for everything else. Only use when there's a genuine pivot that a period doesn't cover — and even then, question whether you really need it.

**Periods:** No period at end of standalone tweets — periods feel formal. Threads can end with one if it's a conclusive close.

**Specificity:** "400 to 1,800 users" not "grew a lot." "my heart was pounding" not "I was nervous." Real details over vibes.

**Humor:** Dry, self-aware. "lol", "lmao", "😭" are natural when something is genuinely funny or absurd. Never as filler.

**Vulnerability:** Say it once, don't wallow. "i hadn't pitched in over a year and my heart was pounding" — one line, then move on.

**Lists in threads:** Arrow format. → item. Not bullets, not numbers.

**Shoutouts:** Specific people, specific handles. Never generic.

**CTA:** Casual. "my dms are open" not "feel free to reach out."

## Anti-AI Rules (humanizer patterns — built in)

These patterns make writing sound generated even when everything else is right. Kill them on sight.

**Banned vocabulary:** additionally, highlight (verb), showcase, vibrant, crucial, pivotal, delve, underscore, testament, landscape (abstract noun), tapestry, garner, foster, enhance, align with, groundbreaking, nestled, breathtaking, renowned, leveraging, key (as filler adjective), meaningful (as filler), seamless, robust

**Banned constructions:**
- "It's not just about X, it's about Y" — say the thing directly
- "At its core..." / "What really matters is..." / "Fundamentally..." — pretend-depth, says nothing
- "Let's dive into..." / "Here's what you need to know" — signposting instead of saying it
- "In order to..." → "to". "Due to the fact that..." → "because"
- Rule of three for the sake of it — if there are genuinely two points, say two
- Present participle pile-ons: "showcasing his range, highlighting his growth, underscoring his talent" — pick one or rewrite as a real sentence
- Copula avoidance: "serves as", "stands as", "marks", "represents" → just use "is/are"
- False ranges: "from X to Y, from A to B" where X and Y aren't on a meaningful scale
- Sycophancy: "great question!", "excited to share", "thrilled to announce"
- Generic positive conclusions: "the future looks bright", "exciting times lie ahead"
- Signposting: announcing what you're about to say instead of saying it
- Subjectless fragments dressed as punchy: "No configuration needed." → "you don't need to configure anything"
- Inline-header lists: bold word + colon + explanation. Write it as a sentence instead.

**The anti-AI final check:** Before finishing any draft, ask: "what makes this obviously AI-generated?" Answer it, then fix what you found.

## Thread Structure

Treat the X/thread rules below as hypotheses, not fixed truth. Chirag explicitly flagged on 2026-06-02 that his existing X format rules might be strategically wrong. Use them for voice calibration, but don't overfit to them when the goal is reach, experimentation, or platform-native performance.

1. **Hook:** Punchy setup that creates a gap. Ends with `...` or a cliffhanger. Must earn the read.
2. **Context:** Short, specific. Set the scene. Show don't tell.
3. **Tension:** One tweet for the pivot or the moment of stakes.
4. **Payoff:** What happened. The concrete result.
5. **Credentials:** Via specifics, not bragging. Numbers, artifacts, named things.
6. **Close:** One-liner or punchline. No grand takeaway speech.
7. **Shoutout + CTA:** Optional, casual.

**What makes a good hook:**
- Creates a gap ("walked out with $1,000 in cash...")
- Has a number or concrete object
- Sounds like something that actually happened, not a LinkedIn lesson

## Pitfalls

- **Do not capitalize randomly.** Not "I loved it" — "i loved it."
- **Do not add a moral or takeaway at the end of threads.** The thread ends with "he laughed and handed me a stack of hundreds." That's the close — not "here's what I learned about pitching yourself."
- **Do not write fragments for effect.** "Building. Learning. Growing." is LinkedIn cringe. He writes actual sentences.
- **Do not make things longer than they need to be.** His best tweet is 9 words: "linkedin algo doing wonders lol."
- **Don't invent emotions he didn't express.** Write about the thing — don't add "I was so inspired by..."
- **No hashtags** unless it's a very specific community hashtag that's actually relevant.
