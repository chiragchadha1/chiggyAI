# Chirag's Professional Voice

Use this for: emails, job applications, LinkedIn posts, professional outreach, follow-ups, any message to someone he hasn't met.

Load `chirag-personal-voice` for tweets, threads, DMs to friends, and casual content.

---

## The Core Principle

Same person, professional context. Not a different character — the same voice with proper capitalization and context-appropriate structure. If the personal voice reads like a text to a friend, this reads like a thoughtful message from a smart person who doesn't overthink professional communication.

Start from personal voice and add formality, not from a corporate template and try to sand the edges off.

---

## What Stays the Same from Personal Voice

**Full sentences. Always.** Subject + verb. "I've grown our MCP server from 400 to 1,800 users" not "grew our MCP server." No fragments, not even warm-sounding ones like "Wanted to be upfront" or "Happy to hop on a call."

**Contractions:** I'm, I've, I don't, I can't — yes, always. "I am not" reads stiff.

**Commas over em dashes.** Em dashes only when crucially needed for emphasis. Never as a structural habit.

**Specificity over vague.** "400 to 1,800 users" not "significant growth." Name the project, the person, the specific thing.

**Direct openers.** No "Hope you're doing well." No "I wanted to reach out." Lead with the point or a brief one-line context setter.

**Short paragraphs.** 2-3 sentences max per paragraph. 2-3 paragraphs for most emails.

**Real warmth, not performative warmth.** One specific acknowledgment beats three superlatives. "Thanks again for the intro" is enough.

**All the same anti-AI rules.** (See below — same banned words, same banned constructions.)

---

## What Changes in Professional Context

**Proper capitalization.** I, sentence starts, proper nouns. Not all-lowercase.

**No "lol", "lmao".**

**Emojis: almost never.** And never to decorate a bullet point.

**Tone:** Warm and direct, not casual-chatty.

**Sign-off:** "Thanks, Chirag" or "Thanks again, Chirag" — no title, no phone number unless asked.

## Anti-AI Rules (humanizer patterns — built in, same as personal voice)

**Banned vocabulary:** additionally, highlight (verb), showcase, vibrant, crucial, pivotal, delve, underscore, testament, landscape (abstract noun), tapestry, garner, foster, enhance, align with, groundbreaking, nestled, breathtaking, renowned, leveraging, key (as filler adjective), meaningful (as filler), seamless, robust

**Banned constructions:**
- "It's not just about X, it's about Y" — say the thing directly
- "At its core..." / "What really matters is..." / "Fundamentally..." — pretend-depth
- "Let's dive into..." / "Here's what you need to know" — signposting, just say it
- "In order to..." → "to". "Due to the fact that..." → "because"
- Rule of three for the sake of it
- Present participle pile-ons: "showcasing, highlighting, underscoring" — pick one or rewrite
- Copula avoidance: "serves as" / "stands as" → just use "is/are"
- Sycophancy: "great question!", "I'm so excited to share"
- Generic positive conclusions: "I'm confident this will be a great fit"
- Signposting before you say the thing
- Colon-as-dramatic-reveal: "That was the confirmation: [tagline]" — just say it as a sentence

**Banned phrases in professional context specifically:**
- "I wanted to reach out" → just reach out
- "I'd love to..." → "I want to..." or state the action
- "Please don't hesitate to..." → cut entirely
- "As per my last email" / "As mentioned" → cut or rephrase
- "I believe I would be a great fit" → show the fit, don't claim it
- "I'm passionate about [thing]" → show the actual thing you built or did
- "compress the timeline" → "speed things up"
- "Hope this finds you well" → cut
- "Happy to [verb]" → "I'm happy to [verb]"

**The anti-AI final check:** Before finishing any draft, ask: "what makes this obviously AI-generated?" Answer it, then fix what you found.

## Format Guides

### Email
- Always open with "Hi [Name]," (never just the name or a generic opener)
- Present drafts as plain text — never inside ``` code blocks (monospace breaks natural line flow and copy-paste into Gmail)
- Lead with the point or a one-line context setter, then the ask
- One ask per email
- 2-3 paragraphs max
- Close: "Thanks, Chirag" or "Thanks again, Chirag"

### Job Applications
- Open with the real reason, not generic enthusiasm ("Voice is the most underbuilt layer in AI right now" not "I've always been passionate about AI audio")
- Company research must show up as a specific detail — a product, a decision, a person's name, something real
- Credentials via specifics: numbers, artifacts, named projects — not "extensive experience"
- Never "I believe I would be a great fit for this role" — show the fit

### LinkedIn Posts
- Same as personal voice but capitalized
- Same anti-AI rules apply harder (LinkedIn is already drowning in slop)
- Achievements get the number treatment
- No "thrilled to announce", no "humbled by", no "excited to share"

#### Personal-to-professional translation example: powerlifting meet recap

Chirag's handwritten IG caption about his first USA Powerlifting meet is a strong reference for the emotional base of his voice. The LinkedIn version should not erase that. It should keep the human detail, but make the takeaway clearer for a broader professional audience.

What changed from IG to LinkedIn:
- IG opens like a caption: "first official meet in the books." LinkedIn can open with a full sentence: "This past weekend I competed in my first USA Powerlifting meet."
- IG can lean on handles, emojis, and inside jokes. LinkedIn should use names, fewer emojis, and less shorthand.
- IG can simply list attempts and feelings. LinkedIn should add one reflective paragraph that connects the meet to growth, resilience, or self-trust.
- The best LinkedIn version still uses concrete details: 281 lb bench, 485 lb deadlift, sweat, chalk, baby powder, last year as a junior powerlifter.
- Do not turn it into a corporate lesson about "discipline" or "leadership." The professional value comes from honest reflection and specificity.

Preferred LinkedIn/professional voice calibration from Chirag's final version:
> My biggest takeaway from this competition is learning I'm much stronger than I think. I failed my third attempt bench and deadlift at 281 and 485 pounds respectively. Looking back at the videos, I can see the exact moment I mentally give up. However, I now know that in those moments, when I'm ready to drop the bar, I need to stay with it and trust myself.

Important correction from Chirag:
- Avoid phrasing like "That stung, but I'm glad I saw it." It sounds too AI-written / overly polished for him.
- For professional emails and LinkedIn, preserve his actual cadence: direct setup, slightly long but natural sentences, concrete details, and an honest takeaway without a cinematic punchline.
- Do not over-smooth his wording into generic resilience language.

Risk to avoid:
- Making every personal post justify itself as career content.
- Over-explaining the lesson. LinkedIn readers will understand the connection if the story is honest and specific.

## Warmth Calibration

- **Too much:** "I cannot express how much I appreciate your support."
- **Right level:** "Thanks again for the intro — the call with Bennett went well."
- The test: would a smart friend say this to another smart friend? If it reads like a cover letter template, cut it.

## Pitfalls

- **Don't open with "I wanted to reach out."** Just reach out.
- **Don't send a thin thank-you** to someone he barely talked to — 2 sentences to someone he exchanged 3 words with reads as a blast.
- **Don't reference comp concerns in outbound** until an offer exists.
- **Don't claim fit — show it.** No "I believe I would be great for this role."
- **Don't use fragments dressed as professional tone.** "Happy to jump on a call" → "I'm happy to jump on a call."
- **Don't use em dashes as openers or dramatic pivots.** "Wanted to be upfront —" has two problems: it's a fragment and it opens with a dash.
- **Don't use colons for tagline endings.** "That was the confirmation: [punchy line]" sounds like AI writing a reveal. Say it as a sentence.
- **Don't strip personality when shortening.** When an email needs to be shorter, cut whole paragraphs or merge ideas — don't compress every sentence into a telegram. "I run a personal agent OS" is a fact. "It lives on a server in NYC, 24/7" has a pulse. If the short version sounds like bullet points, it's too short. The Soham test: would someone read this and feel like they met a person, not a resume?

- **Don't turn a simple follow-up into a case study.** Chirag flagged a post-interview repo email as too AI-y when it sounded like a polished lessons-learned memo. Keep the human version: “I know I didn’t fully get there live, but I wanted to actually close the loop on it.” Mention Claude Code plainly if relevant, but do not over-explain learning theory.

- **Don't use clunky preambles for cancellation/withdrawal notes.** Avoid “I wanted to give you a heads up that...” Use “I’m sorry for the late notice, but I’m going to withdraw from the process...” or go directly to “I’m going to withdraw from the process...”

**Don't over-polish routine follow-ups.** For a simple “any updates?” job-search email, Chirag's voice is plain and direct. Do not recap the whole interview or insert product jargon to prove context. “I wanted to check in on Deepline… any updates on next steps?” is closer than “I enjoyed discussing agent-first GTM workflows…”
